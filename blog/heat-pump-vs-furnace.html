Heat Pump vs Furnace: Which Is Right for Your Ontario Home?

Compare heat pumps and furnaces for cost, comfort and efficiency in Ontario winters, and learn when a hybrid system makes sense.

By Jora Mechanical Systems • October 2, 2026

The basic difference

A furnace creates heat by burning fuel, usually natural gas. A heat pump moves existing heat from one place to another using electricity. In summer it works like an air conditioner, and in winter it runs in reverse.

Do heat pumps work in Canadian winters?

Modern cold-climate heat pumps are designed to keep providing heat at temperatures well below freezing. Performance and efficiency do drop as it gets colder, which is why many homes pair a heat pump with a furnace as a hybrid system.

Running costs

Natural gas is often cheaper per unit of energy than electricity, but a heat pump delivers much more heat per unit of energy used. Which one costs less to run depends on gas and electricity rates, your home's insulation and the equipment you choose.

Upfront cost and incentives

Heat pumps typically cost more to install than a furnace alone, though they replace both heating and cooling equipment. Government and utility incentives change over time, so check what is currently available before you decide.

Which should you choose?

A furnace is a proven, lower-cost choice for heating-only needs. A heat pump makes sense if you also need a new air conditioner or want to reduce gas use. A hybrid combines both. A free in-home assessment will tell you what fits your house.
